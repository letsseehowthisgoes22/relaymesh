When cellular service or the internet fails, ordinary messages stop. RelayMesh lets encrypted messages find another path. The sender enters a message and a recovery phrase. The browser derives an AES 256 key, encrypts locally, and splits the ciphertext into five shuffled fragments. Each fragment moves through a different opt-in relay. Relays never see the plaintext or the whole message. The receiver uses rotating rendezvous tags derived from the same phrase, collects the fragments, verifies their integrity, and decrypts locally. After successful delivery, the receiver signs a receipt. RelayEscrow then releases RelayCoin to the devices that performed useful work. No delivery, no reward. This MVP performs real encryption, fragmentation, tamper rejection, reconstruction, and reward simulation. Next, the same protocol moves onto Bluetooth, Wi-Fi Direct, libp2p, and LoRa. RelayMesh. Messages find a way, even when networks fail.
